# Task 5: Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR)

## 1. Introduction

An Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) is a structured report of a
suspected adverse event or adverse drug reaction (ADR) in an individual
patient after exposure to a medicinal product. ICSRs are an important
part of pharmacovigilance because they help healthcare professionals and
medicine regulators identify, assess, and monitor potential
medicine-related risks.

## 2. Objectives

-   Identify the suspected adverse drug reaction.
-   Collect and organise the minimum information needed for a valid
    ICSR.
-   Assess the temporal relationship between the medicine and the
    reaction.
-   Evaluate causality using a recognised method.
-   Classify the event by seriousness and expectedness.
-   Recommend appropriate clinical and pharmacovigilance actions.

## 3. Minimum Criteria for a Valid ICSR

A valid report generally requires four core elements:

1.  An identifiable patient.
2.  An identifiable reporter.
3.  A suspected medicinal product.
4.  A suspected adverse reaction or event.

Patient and reporter details should be handled confidentially. Do not
put identifiable patient information in a public GitHub repository.

## 5. ADR Identification and Clinical Assessment

Rash and itching may be associated with amoxicillin exposure, but the
timing alone does not prove that the medicine caused the event. Other
possible causes, including the underlying infection and other medicines,
should be considered.

**Information to collect** - Date and time the medicine was started. -
Date and time the reaction began. - Dose, route, indication, and any
recent dose changes. - Description, distribution, and progression of the
rash. - Previous allergy history and previous exposure to penicillins. -
Concomitant medicines and relevant illnesses. - Treatment given for the
reaction and the patient's outcome. - Whether the medicine was stopped
and whether the reaction improved. - Whether the reaction recurred after
re-exposure; intentional rechallenge should not be performed solely to
test causality.

## 6. Causality Assessment

Causality assessment estimates how likely it is that a medicine caused a
suspected reaction. It does not prove causation.

A recognised approach is the **WHO--UMC causality assessment system**.
The assessor considers the timing, alternative explanations, response to
withdrawal, previous knowledge of the reaction, and available clinical
evidence.

For this illustrative case, the causality category is
**unassessable/unclassifiable with the information provided**, because
onset dates, clinical details, dechallenge information, and alternative
causes are missing. Do not assign "certain" or "probable" without
supporting evidence.

### Information that would strengthen the assessment

-   A plausible time relationship between starting amoxicillin and rash
    onset.
-   Clinical description and assessment of the reaction.
-   Improvement after withdrawal, where withdrawal occurred for clinical
    reasons.
-   Evaluation of alternative causes.
-   Relevant allergy history and other medicines.

## 7. Seriousness and Expectedness Classification

### Seriousness

An event is serious if it results in death, is life-threatening,
requires or prolongs hospitalisation, causes significant disability,
results in a congenital anomaly, or is otherwise medically important.

For this example, seriousness cannot be confirmed from the short
description alone. If the rash is mild, limited to the skin, and has no
serious features, it is generally non-serious. Facial or tongue
swelling, breathing difficulty, collapse, blistering, or mucosal
involvement requires urgent medical assessment.

### Expectedness

Expectedness is determined by comparing the reaction's nature and
severity with the applicable current approved product information. Rash
and itching may be listed for amoxicillin, but the exact label must be
checked before recording the final classification.

## 8. Recommended Pharmacist Actions

1.  Review the patient promptly and assess for signs of a severe
    allergic reaction.
2.  Escalate urgently if there is breathing difficulty, facial/tongue
    swelling, collapse, blistering, or mucosal involvement.
3.  Inform the treating clinician and discuss whether amoxicillin should
    be withheld or changed based on the clinical presentation.
4.  Record the suspected reaction accurately in the medical record.
5.  Document the suspected medicine, dose, dates, reaction details,
    seriousness, treatment, and outcome.
6.  Report the suspected ADR through the applicable pharmacovigilance
    system and follow local reporting requirements.
7.  Provide appropriate counselling about allergy documentation and when
    to seek emergency care.

## 9. ICSR Reporting Checklist

Before finalising a report, check that the following have been completed
where information is available:

-   [ ] Patient identifiable within the reporting system (without public
    disclosure of identity).
-   [ ] Reporter identifiable within the reporting system.
-   [ ] Suspected medicine details recorded.
-   [ ] Reaction description and onset recorded.
-   [ ] Seriousness criteria assessed.
-   [ ] Expectedness checked against approved product information.
-   [ ] Relevant medical history and concomitant medicines recorded.
-   [ ] Causality assessment documented with its rationale.
-   [ ] Treatment, action taken, and outcome recorded.
-   [ ] Missing information clearly marked as unknown/not reported.
-   [ ] Confidentiality protected.
-   [ ] Report submitted according to applicable local procedures.

## 10. Conclusion

An ICSR supports systematic collection and assessment of suspected
medicine-related safety events. A high-quality report includes clear
reaction details, suspected medicine information, seriousness and
expectedness assessments, a reasoned causality evaluation, and follow-up
information. The available details in this training brief are
insufficient to establish causality or a final seriousness
classification, so missing data should be obtained rather than assumed.
